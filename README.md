<h2>Nexus Setups | Website and Business Platform Planning</h2>

This portfolio covers the Nexus Setups project, completed by a two-member team over approximately six months, from 1 April to 1 October 2026. Trello was used to organise the work into sprint lists and record user stories and task descriptions. We completed the tasks on the board, working through changing requirements and time constraints along the way. The Trello export contains 85 cards across 13 sprint lists, covering business planning, branding, the public website, and the wider digital platform.

<h2>1. Agile Team Charter</h2>

<h3>Project vision</h3>

The project vision is to establish a professional digital presence for Nexus Setups and organise the planned services and supporting business processes. The Trello board begins with the business vision, mission, business model, competitor research, target audience, value proposition, revenue model, pricing, and success measures. It then moves into company setup and brand identity before describing a website and a wider client-facing platform for managing projects and services.

<h3>Shared goal</h3>

The shared goal is to deliver a coherent Nexus Setups website and a clearly organised plan for the wider digital service experience. The public website should explain the business, present its services and work, provide useful information to visitors, and support enquiries. The wider Trello plan also identifies future-facing capabilities such as user accounts, a client dashboard, project submissions, file delivery, revision requests, billing, administration, and launch monitoring. These are recorded as planned backlog items unless separate implementation evidence confirms that they were delivered.

<h3>Project scope represented on Trello</h3>

- **Business planning:** vision, mission, business model, competitor analysis, SWOT analysis, audience, value proposition, revenue model, pricing, and KPIs.
- **Company formation:** business and tax registration, business banking, insurance, domain and hosting, professional email, and accounting software.
- **Branding:** logo, brand guidelines, colour palette, typography, business cards, email signatures, and social media branding.
- **Public website:** homepage, About, Services, Portfolio, Pricing, Contact, FAQ, blog, SEO, accessibility, performance, and analytics.
- **Client platform planning:** registration and authentication, account security, client dashboard, project overview, notifications, downloads, profile management, billing, and activity.
- **Operational workflows:** project submission and tracking, asset uploads, editor assignments, deadlines, editing workflow, revisions, quality assurance, and delivery.
- **Business operations and growth:** communication, invoicing and payments, marketing, administration, beta testing, documentation, monitoring, and launch.

<h3>Working agreements</h3>

- Keep the Trello board organised so that planned work is visible.
- Break large objectives into specific cards that can be reviewed individually.
- Use user stories to connect tasks to a founder, visitor, client, editor, manager, marketer, or administrator need.
- Agree on priorities before committing work to a sprint.
- Communicate blockers early and record decisions that affect scope or delivery.
- Review the outcome of tasks rather than treating a card title alone as evidence of completion.
- Keep business branding, website content, navigation, and service descriptions consistent.
- Consider security, accessibility, usability, and performance alongside visual design.
- Record completed work and outstanding work accurately, and attach screenshots, links, or other evidence where available.
- Review lessons at the end of a sprint and use them to improve the next planning cycle.

<h3>Team responsibilities</h3>

Nexus Setups was completed by a **two-member team**. We used Trello to organise the project, divide the work into manageable tasks, and keep the different areas of the project visible.

- **Planning and organisation:** Use the sprint lists, user stories, and task descriptions to organise the work and clarify what each task is intended to achieve.
- **Branding and website:** Develop a consistent visual identity and a clear public-facing website for Nexus Setups.
- **Content and requirements:** Gather the information needed for the website and translate project needs into user stories and task descriptions.
- **Testing and review:** Check the live website, identify issues, and review whether the result meets the intended requirements.
- **Communication and presentations:** Keep each other informed about progress and changes, and communicate the project outcomes.

Tasks were handled individually or together depending on the work involved. The work represented by the Trello cards was completed, including the tasks across the different sprint areas.

<h3>Definition of done</h3>

A card was treated as complete when its intended result had been produced and reviewed. Where applicable, the work was checked against the relevant requirement or user story, and issues identified during testing were reviewed. Although the exported CSV does not include status fields, the project team confirms that the tasks on the Trello board were completed.

<h3>Reflection on the team charter</h3>

The project showed how important planning and communication are, especially when requirements change and time is limited. Trello helped organise the different parts of the project, but keeping track of tasks and priorities was not always easy. In future projects, I would improve my time management by breaking work into smaller steps, setting clearer priorities, and reviewing progress more regularly.

<h3>Logbook habits for future projects</h3>

- Record the sprint goal at the start of the sprint.
- Note which cards were completed, which remain in progress, and which were blocked.
- Record decisions, scope changes, and reasons for moving work between sprints.
- Capture screenshots, links, or test evidence for important deliverables.
- Write a short retrospective with what went well, what caused difficulty, and one or more specific improvements.
- Keep the dates and status fields in Trello updated so the final portfolio can reflect the actual work rather than relying on memory.
